# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Primarily everyday people and households who need help with technology but feel intimidated by it or talked down to elsewhere — often older or less tech-confident visitors dealing with a slow computer, a broken printer, a dead hard drive, or a box of old tapes they want to save. Small businesses needing ongoing, personal IT support are a secondary audience with the same expectation: no jargon, no call centre.

## Product Purpose

CantyTech's site introduces Matt's one-person tech services business — plain-English IT help, AV-to-digital conversion, data recovery, and custom software/scripting — and gets visitors comfortable enough to email directly. Success is a visitor understanding what's offered without needing to already know the terminology, and trusting the person behind it enough to reach out.

## Positioning

The technology help you'd get from a knowledgeable friend, not a call centre: one person, explaining things in plain English, who you can actually reach and trust with your devices and data — not a faceless chain or an outsourced queue.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: email directly (mailto). No secondary CTA — one clear action, not a menu of options.
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: "Tech help from a real person, explained simply."
- Belief ladder: (1) this person understands my actual problem, not just the technical version of it; (2) I can trust them with my devices and my data; (3) reaching out is easy and low-pressure — just an email, no sales pitch; (4) they specifically do the thing I need (IT help, digitising old tapes/photos, recovering lost files, or a custom script/tool).
- Proof on hand: none yet. Ship with boilerplate trust cues (clear service descriptions, direct personal tone) rather than fabricated testimonials or credentials — swap in real testimonials, credentials, or past-work examples once supplied.

## Brand Personality

Approachable, expert, trustworthy. Warm without being cutesy; confident without being corporate. The voice of someone who's good at this and enjoys explaining it, not someone reciting a spec sheet.

## Anti-references

Not a generic dark-mode SaaS/startup site (gradient hero, glassy cards, "move your business forward" copy) — that reads cold and enterprise, wrong for a personal local service. Not a big-chain repair shop or outsourced-support tone (queue numbers, ticket systems, scripted corporate copy). Avoid jargon in any visitor-facing copy, even when describing genuinely technical services.

## Design Principles

- Plain English first: describe every service in terms of the visitor's problem, never the technical mechanism, without dumbing it down.
- One person, not a company: the site should feel like meeting Matt, not filling out a request to "a team."
- Clarity beats cleverness: obvious navigation, generous type, low cognitive load — designed for visitors who may not be confident with technology.
- Reassurance over persuasion: the job of the page is to lower anxiety about handing over a device or old memories, not to hard-sell.
- Show breadth without diluting focus: IT help leads, but AV digitising, data recovery, and custom software all read as credible, related expertise — not a scattered list.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Design specifically for older and less tech-confident visitors: strong contrast, comfortably large body text, very clear and predictable navigation, minimal reliance on hover/gesture-only interaction, and no dense or jargon-heavy blocks of text. General WCAG AA practice as the floor, biased further toward legibility and simplicity than a typical product audience would need.
